The freehold versus leasehold question comes up in almost every conversation I have with first-time buyers in Bali. Most people arrive with a view formed elsewhere, usually from property markets where freehold is always better and leasehold is a compromise. In Bali, that framing leads you to the wrong conclusion.

Let me clear it up properly.

## What Freehold Actually Means in Indonesia

In Indonesia, the highest form of land title is called Hak Milik. It is permanent ownership, fully registered at the national land registry (BPN), and transferable between Indonesian citizens indefinitely. It is the equivalent of freehold in the conventional sense.

Here is the catch: foreigners cannot hold Hak Milik. It is legally restricted to Indonesian citizens. If you are an expat buying property in Bali, you simply cannot own freehold land in that sense. Full stop.

What you can do as a foreigner is establish an Indonesian foreign-owned company, known as a PT PMA. A PT PMA can hold a different category of title called HGB (Hak Guna Bangunan), which is a right to build and use land for 30 years, extendable to 80 years in total. This is what most people mean when they refer to "freehold via PT PMA" in the Bali market. It is a company-held title with a defined duration, not Hak Milik, but it is registered at BPN and provides a higher level of legal formality than a leasehold contract.

So when someone tells you a property is available as freehold, and you are a foreigner, what they usually mean is that the structure supports PT PMA with HGB title. That is different from a private individual holding freehold land.

## What Leasehold Actually Means in Bali

Leasehold in Bali, technically Hak Sewa, is a civil law contract between a buyer and a landowner. You are purchasing the right to use and occupy land and buildings for a defined period, typically 25 to 30 years upfront with a contractual right to renew for another 25 to 30 years, giving you an effective 50 to 80 year hold.

The critical legal point that a lot of people miss: a leasehold agreement is NOT registered at BPN. It is a notarial deed, executed before a licensed Indonesian notary (PPAT), but it sits outside the formal BPN land registry system. That is not the same as being unenforceable. Notarial deeds are legally valid instruments under Indonesian civil law. But the protections are different from a BPN-registered title, and the strength of your position depends heavily on the quality of the contract itself.

A well-structured lease will include a clear renewal clause, a non-disturbance clause protecting your possession through any change of ownership of the underlying land, a prohibition on the landowner encumbering the land without your consent, and provisions for what happens if the landowner dies. A poorly structured lease has none of those things, and then you have a problem at year 25. This is why the lawyer you use matters as much as the property you buy.

## The ROI Maths

Here is where leasehold stops looking like a compromise and starts looking like a strategy.

Assume two identical villas side by side in Canggu. One is available as leasehold for $320,000. The other, structured for PT PMA with HGB title, is available for $480,000. The rental income is the same: both sit in the same market, attract the same guests, and charge the same nightly rate.

At Canggu's average gross yield of 14.8%, the leasehold villa generates roughly $47,360 per year in gross rental income. On a $320K entry price, that is a 14.8% gross yield. On the $480K freehold structure, the same income represents a 9.9% gross yield. The asset pays the same rent. Your return depends entirely on what you paid for it.

That 30 to 40% price differential between leasehold and freehold structured properties is consistent across the market. Leasehold is cheaper to enter because the asset is time-limited. But in a market where properties generate 10 to 15% gross yields and buildings degrade in a tropical climate, that lower entry price is frequently the smarter position.

## The Tropical Climate Argument

Bali is hard on buildings. The combination of humidity, salt air in coastal areas, UV exposure, and a rainy season that delivers 2,000mm of rainfall a year means that even well-maintained villas age faster than equivalent property in temperate climates. In my experience, a villa built today in Bali will require substantial renovation or refurbishment within 15 to 20 years to maintain its rental appeal.

This changes the calculus on a 50-year leasehold versus 80-year HGB freehold. For most investors, the practical life of the asset at current specification is 20 to 30 years before significant capital expenditure is needed. A 30-year initial lease term with a renewal clause covers that useful life and then some. You are not actually giving up that much by not owning in perpetuity.

## Who Should Use PT PMA

A PT PMA makes genuine sense in specific situations. If you are building a portfolio of two or more properties, the PT PMA structure allows you to hold multiple assets under one company, which simplifies compliance and accounting. If you are operating a formal hospitality business with staff, a PT PMA with the correct KBLI licensing is required. If you have a 30-plus year horizon and want BPN-registered title for the security it provides, PT PMA with HGB is the appropriate vehicle. And if you are acquiring higher-value commercial assets above $600K to $800K where the title formality justifies the setup cost, it makes sense.

For a single investment villa, first-time buyers, or anyone with a hold period under 25 years, leasehold via notary is the more efficient and more profitable structure.

## Exit Strategy on a Leasehold

One concern I hear regularly: what happens when you want to sell? The answer is straightforward. You sell the remaining lease. If you bought a 30-year leasehold with a 30-year renewal clause and want to exit after 8 years, you are selling 22 years remaining plus the contractual right to a further 30. That is still a highly marketable asset. Buyers in Bali buy remaining leaseholds constantly. The secondary leasehold market is active and liquid at the quality end of the market.

The key is the renewal clause. A lease with a clean, unconditional right to renew is worth significantly more on resale than one where renewal is at the landowner's discretion. Get this right at the time of purchase and it protects your exit as much as your entry.

## The Bottom Line

For most foreign investors in Bali, leasehold is not a second-best option. It is the structure that delivers the highest returns, fits the asset's actual useful life, and avoids the overhead of running an Indonesian company. Understand what you are buying, use a proper lawyer, and get the renewal clause right.
