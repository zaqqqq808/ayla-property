Let me get the confession out of the way first. Every gym on this list is better than almost any gym you have ever trained in. The one in last place would be the best gym in most cities on earth. I have trained in those cities, under flickering strip lights, next to a water cooler that gave up years ago, and I would have walked a long way in the heat to reach the worst room in this article.

All eight of them sit within about ten minutes of each other. We are spoiled here, and I know it.

But a critic who loves everything is no use to anybody. When you live with this much choice, the small things start to matter, and nobody on this list gets all of them right. Nobody comes close.

I trained at every gym here for a full week. I have lived in Bali for more than three years. I lift like a bodybuilder, I am fussier about recovery than my friends can stand, and I actually enjoy a class. If someone was going to write the definitive Bali gym review, it was probably going to be someone like me. So here it is.

We start at the bottom and climb. The top three are worth the wait.

## 8. Undefeated, 6.3/10

**A small gym with the right machines, and not much else.**

**Location and the building.** Undefeated sits in Tumbuk Bayuh, a little away from the main hustle, though never far enough to complain about. Anime artwork runs along the walls and does more work than you would expect. It gives the place its whole personality, a neon, after-midnight mood that would not look out of place in Akira. It is also the smallest room on this list, which is the plain reason space scores a 3. The layout is fine. There is just less of it.

**What it gets right.** The machines are the ones a bodybuilder actually wants, which puts Undefeated in the same conversation as Reload and Obsidian II. The recovery is indoors. In this climate that sounds like a mistake, and it turns out to work, with a futuristic look that suits the rest of the building. The pool round the back is decent. Nothing special, though better than Rite Gym's. And as I write this the place is rarely busy, which at five o'clock on a Tuesday afternoon is worth more than marble.

**What it gets wrong.** The food is limited and there is nowhere to sit and work. I suspect that is deliberate. This is a place that would quite like you to leave when you are finished. There are no classes at all. Community scores a 2, the lowest here, and that is the price of all that quiet. You will train, shower and go home without speaking to a soul.

**My take.** I have always had a soft spot for the small film that knows exactly what it is and has no interest in becoming anything else. Undefeated is that film. If quiet and out of the way is what you want, this is your gym, and I mean that sincerely. But a table that rewards breadth was always going to put it last. Even if you are a serious bodybuilder, I would send you to Obsidian II first.

**Score:** Equipment 4, Space 3, Hygiene 4, Recovery 4, Location 3, Restaurant 3, Vibes 4, Extras 2, Building 4, Combat 2, Community 2, Pool 3, Classes 0, Price 5.

## 7. Obsidian II, 7.2/10

**A warehouse gym comes to Bali, with some of the best gym equipment on the island.**

**Location and the building.** Easy to reach, and set just off a busy main road. That would be a safety problem if it were not for the men with red glow sticks waving you in and out like ground crew on an aircraft carrier. Inside, it is one big warehouse room with everything in it, calisthenics rig included, and that works better than it sounds. The headline is the kit. Obsidian II has Panatta machines, widely considered the best made gym machines in the world, and they know it. It is on their billboards.

What the building lacks is character. Things have been added over the years without much of a plan. The water slide is the clearest example, and I am still not sure who it is for. Vibes scores a 2, the lowest on this list, and the building a 3.

**What it gets right.** The equipment is among the best in Bali, in a room big enough to use it properly. Serious kit draws serious regulars, which is why community lands on a 4. Anyone who has seen Pumping Iron will recognise the atmosphere. People come here to work, and the small talk can wait. The restaurant is one of only two 5s for food on this list, with a big menu, a build your own option and a strong run of protein desserts. The cave style hot pool is a good touch.

**What it gets wrong.** The calisthenics area is small and indoors. This is the tropics. That part of a gym belongs outside, with some fans and some shade, partly for the air and partly for the change of scene, because training under air conditioning every single day wears on you. Every other gym here has an outdoor calisthenics space. Obsidian II should too. The recovery area already looks tired, in a building that is not old. The outdoor showers have no gel or shampoo. The changing rooms are grim, with nowhere to sit and nowhere to put anything down, and that is what drags the combined space score to a 3. Classes barely exist in public. Hyrox and yoga are the only two I could find named anywhere, so the 3 reflects what I could verify. And at roughly $172 a month, it is not cheap.

**My take.** Obsidian II gets the small things wrong, and I doubt that keeps anyone there awake at night. It fits the hardcore, no nonsense appeal of the place. For a certain kind of lifter that is a fair trade, and that lifter would not thank me for suggesting a scented candle.

**Score:** Equipment 5, Space 3, Hygiene 4, Recovery 3, Location 4, Restaurant 5, Vibes 2, Extras 3, Building 3, Combat 4, Community 4, Pool 3, Classes 3, Price 4.

## 6. Nirvana, 7.2/10

**A social club that happens to own dumbbells.**

**Location and the building.** The location is poor. Nirvana sits in the thick of the tourist strip, which is part of why it is always full, and the drive in is a chore that the car park then makes worse. The building is unremarkable, with no architectural idea behind it. Both score a 3, and neither is the reason anybody joins.

**What it gets right.** The people. Community is a 5, the only one on this list. Arrive in Bali knowing nobody and within a week you will have found your crowd here. It is the closest thing Canggu has to the bar in Cheers. The recovery is excellent, one of only two 5s in that category. And the timetable is enormous, comfortably past a hundred sessions a week, with real range across vinyasa, power, yin and hatha yoga, pilates, sculpt, barre, splits, handstands and dance styles nobody else here would attempt.

**What it gets wrong.** The equipment is the weakest on this list. You can train on it, but if you lift seriously you will find the ceiling quickly. There is no combat of any kind. And for all that volume, there is not one class billed as HIIT and nothing boxing adjacent, which is why classes lands on a 4.

**My take.** Some films are made for critics and some are made for an audience, and with the second kind the audience is usually right. Nirvana picked its audience, the Berawa wellness crowd who care more about feeling well than about weights and aesthetics, and built something exactly for them. That is a strategy, and it is working. It finishes sixth, 0.2 of a point clear of Obsidian II. If you are new here and know nobody, ignore the ranking entirely.

**Score:** Equipment 3, Space 4, Hygiene 4, Recovery 5, Location 3, Restaurant 4, Vibes 3, Extras 4, Building 3, Combat 2, Community 5, Pool 3, Classes 4, Price 3.

## 5. Surya Gym, 7.5/10

**The best fight focused gym on this list, with a recovery experience you will not find anywhere else.**

**Location and the building.** A decent location, off the main road like Obsidian II. The same road, in fact, about 100 metres further down. What Surya has is space, which never shows up on a website and which everybody notices the moment they walk in.

**What it gets right.** The fighting. A full [fight club programme](https://www.suryagymbali.com/fight-club/), a large mat area to roll and spar on, its own octagon, and classes from six in the morning until nine at night. It is the only 5 for combat on this list. The rooftop recovery is the other reason to come. You sit in the pool while the sun goes down over Canggu, directly above the octagon, and watch the sparring below. It is a bit like having a box at the opera, if the opera were two men trying to take each other's backs. The calisthenics area is generous and the pool tables on the ground floor are a nice touch.

**What it gets wrong.** The equipment is good and old, and the age shows now that the competition unwraps new machines every couple of years. The changing rooms are dingy and not clean enough for the climate or the price. The food is limited. And almost nothing has been built around the gym itself beyond the café style co working on the ground floor, which is how a gym this good at one thing ends up with a 2 for extras.

**My take.** Surya is a specialist and should be judged as one. Its fight club is also its entire class timetable, so the combat score and the classes score are the same asset seen from two angles. I want to be precise about what it offers. If you want to train alongside genuinely world class fighters, dedicated fight gyms like Soma and Bali MMA are grittier, simpler and better at exactly that. What Surya gives you is a proper gym with a serious fight programme attached, and on those terms nothing else in Canggu comes close. Fifth overall, and the extras column is the only thing keeping it out of the top four.

**Score:** Equipment 4, Space 4, Hygiene 4, Recovery 4, Location 4, Restaurant 4, Vibes 3, Extras 2, Building 5, Combat 5, Community 3, Pool 3, Classes 4, Price 3.

## 4. Rite Gym, 7.7/10

**The best building on this list, in the best location.**

**Location and the building.** Brutalist concrete by the Indonesian studio 2M, done with conviction and with the proportions properly thought through. If you saw The Brutalist you will recognise the ambition, though this building is a good deal more pleasant to spend an afternoon in. It sits a short walk from the sand in [Pererenan](/invest/pererenan/), close enough to finish a session and jog straight into the sea. The best location here, by a comfortable margin.

**What it gets right.** The recovery is the only one in Canggu aiming for calm. Quiet music, a Japanese style garden, a secluded ice bath, oils in the steam room. Everywhere else is trying to turn recovery into a beach club, and Bali already has plenty of those. The classes share the top score on this list, a 5, covering boxing and muay thai, HYROX, 5x5 metcon, a calisthenics and handstand programme nobody else runs, yoga and pilates twice a day, plus sound baths and breathwork. The new boxing room is superb. And the details land. There are q-tips, micellar water and hand lotion in the changing rooms. Somebody here has thought about what it actually feels like to be a member.

**What it gets wrong.** They book yoga into that boxing room, so much of the time you cannot use it, and they do the same upstairs by running classes in the space people are trying to train in. Equipment is a 4, good without being memorable, spread across the roof, a dedicated booty room and new machines downstairs. I preferred the ground floor when it was where the heavy deadlifts happened. It now sits largely unused. Hygiene was poor the last few times I went. The dark changing rooms hide dirt, and there are no spray wipes on the gym floor. The food is the lowest restaurant score on this list, and you eat it in the evening while the mosquitoes eat you.

**My take.** Rite was my favourite gym for years, so read all of that as family criticism, the kind you only bother with when you care. It is still the only place in Canggu where somebody has thought about what the building smells like. Fourth, 0.2 of a point behind House of Kairos. Renovations are under way, I expect this score to move by Christmas, and I will be back to check.

**Score:** Equipment 4, Space 4, Hygiene 3, Recovery 4, Location 5, Restaurant 2, Vibes 5, Extras 4, Building 5, Combat 4, Community 3, Pool 3, Classes 5, Price 3.

## 3. House of Kairos, 7.7/10

**The closest thing Bali has to a Soho House, with a few squat racks in it.**

**Location and the building.** A great spot, just down the road from Open House, and then the most beautiful gym on the island by a distance. Art deco through the restaurant and the co working space, a spiral staircase, and a level of finish nothing else here approaches. Wes Anderson could shoot a film in here without moving a chair. It feels less like a membership than a club.

**What it gets right.** The pool is extraordinary, the best I have seen at any gym in Bali and the only 5 in that column, with the best loungers too. The food is exceptional, gym focused and properly cooked, closer to a good restaurant than a gym kitchen. The co working space is handsome and usable. The gym floor is spotless. And there is a genuinely cool touch in the restaurant, a pair of DJ decks and a vinyl collection, perfectly in keeping with the art deco.

**What it gets wrong.** There is no combat. Not a heavy bag, not even a light one. The equipment is brand new Technogym, more than enough for almost everybody and slightly short for the genuinely strong, who will find its ceiling. The steam room was grubby when I used it, which in a building this considered is as jarring as a typo on a wedding invitation. There is nowhere to hang a towel at the outdoor showers, and no shampoo when you get there. The timetable is thin, around thirteen sessions across five weekdays, members only and booked through the app. Community is a 3, a beautiful room full of people enjoying themselves separately. And it sits in the expensive tier, at a price it does not publish.

**My take.** House of Kairos had every chance to win this and lost it on small things. I would also like to see the macros on the menu. Third, 0.8 of a point behind Reload and 0.2 ahead of Rite Gym, the tightest corner of this table.

**Score:** Equipment 4, Space 4, Hygiene 5, Recovery 4, Location 5, Restaurant 4, Vibes 5, Extras 4, Building 5, Combat 0, Community 3, Pool 5, Classes 3, Price 2.

## 2. Reload, 7.8/10

**The gym that should have won.**

**Location and the building.** Easy to get to. You arrive past a rail of clothes and a cabinet of jewellery, which makes the entrance feel more like a leisure centre than a gym. The rooftop is superb and the main room is big. Underneath all that it feels cheaply built, and the stairwell is grim.

**What it gets right.** The machines are brand new and of very high quality, some of them better than Obsidian II's, with ergonomics that suggest an actual lifter was consulted. If the quality of the iron is what matters most to you, you can stop reading here. The restaurant is superb and would survive on its own with no gym attached. Build your own or à la carte, macros counted, and enough variety to eat there four times a week. It is one of only two 5s for food on this list. The pool is a solid 4. And the class timetable is properly varied, with Hyrox most days, boxing, a serious pilates rotation across four formats, and enough yoga and mobility to fill out the week.

**What it gets wrong.** Recovery is a 3, joint worst here. The steam room is a mess and has been out of action for months. There is no shower gel at the showers, so washing properly means a walk round the back, past the bins. The woodwork around the recovery area is already coming apart. The blue evening lighting flatters nobody. The punch bags hang in an awkward spot where the flooring changes, which makes working them for any length of time difficult. And the artwork is poor throughout, including a computer generated woman staring into the middle distance, a tacky thing to hang in a room this expensive. Small details make a big difference, and Reload glosses over them.

**My take.** Every so often a film arrives with a huge budget, a great cast and a magnificent set, and somewhere in the edit nobody noticed the boom mic hanging into the shot. Reload is that film. It could have been the best gym on this list, and it has real flashes of it. They are reaching for a higher tier of gym experience and in most respects they have got there. What they keep missing are the small things Omni and Rite Gym get right, and the small things are what separate a great gym from an expensive one. Second, 6.8 points behind Omni and 0.8 clear of House of Kairos. They are expanding, so there is time.

**Score:** Equipment 5, Space 4, Hygiene 5, Recovery 3, Location 5, Restaurant 5, Vibes 3, Extras 3, Building 4, Combat 3, Community 4, Pool 4, Classes 4, Price 2.

## 1. Omni, 8.7/10

**The most complete gym in Canggu, and aptly named.**

**Location and the building.** The location is fine, out at the end of the Seseh road just past the traffic lights. The building makes up for it, well made and stylish without being pleased with itself. Most gyms empty out at six. Omni looks even better in the evening.

**What it gets right.** There is no weak category anywhere on the card. Space 5, hygiene 5, recovery 5. Those are three of the four most heavily weighted columns, and nobody else here manages even two of them. The changing rooms are probably the best on this list along with House of Kairos, modern and clean. The infrared is a nice touch. The co working is the best in Canggu, and I mean the best co working in Canggu, full stop, as opposed to the best one bolted onto a gym. There are two separate calisthenics areas, so a class taking over one does not cost you your session, which is the most considerate piece of gym design in this town. The timetable covers more ground than anyone else's, more than 65 sessions a week across just about every style going. The food is good, with build your own. And there are two outdoor training areas, the obviously correct call in this climate.

**What it gets wrong.** The equipment is a 4, roughly level with Rite Gym and nowhere near Obsidian II, so the purists will always have somewhere else to be. Combat is a 3. And it is expensive, in the top price tier with Reload and House of Kairos.

**My take.** Howard Hawks used to say a good movie needs "three great scenes and no bad ones." The second half of that is the hard part, and it is where Omni wins. It never gives you a reason to complain, which is harder to say about anywhere else on this page. Every other gym here has a hole in it somewhere. Omni has a combat score of 3, iron that is merely good, and a price that knows what it is. That is the full list of grievances. It wins by 6.8 points, the second biggest gap in the table after the one between seventh and last, because it remains the only gym in Canggu that is bad at nothing.

**Score:** Equipment 4, Space 5, Hygiene 5, Recovery 5, Location 4, Restaurant 4, Vibes 5, Extras 5, Building 5, Combat 3, Community 4, Pool 4, Classes 5, Price 2.

## So which one should you actually join

- **You want the fewest compromises:** Omni. It is first for a reason, and by the biggest margin near the top of the table.
- **You have just arrived and know nobody:** Nirvana. Sixth on this list, and the right answer for you by a mile.
- **You fight, or want to:** Surya Gym, if you want a full gym wrapped around the fight programme. If fighting is the whole point, go to a dedicated fight gym like Soma or Bali MMA instead.
- **You train in the morning and work all afternoon:** Omni. The co working alone justifies it.
- **You actually swim:** House of Kairos, then Omni or Reload. Nothing else here has a pool worth planning your day around.
- **You want to feel like your life is going well:** House of Kairos.
- **You only care about the machines:** Reload, then Obsidian II, then Undefeated.
- **You want the widest class timetable:** Omni or Rite Gym, both a 5 for classes, though Omni has far more volume behind it.
- **You live in Pererenan:** Rite Gym. Fourth on this list, and the renovation may well push it higher by Christmas.
- **You want to train hard, cheaply, with no queue:** Undefeated.
- **You want the cheapest membership on the list:** Undefeated, by a wide margin, then Obsidian II.

Which brings me to the real lesson of this list. The winner is the gym with no weaknesses, but the right gym for you is usually the one that is brilliant at the single thing you care about. A critic ranks things. You only have to choose one. Nirvana is sixth, and it is still the best answer for a lot of people reading this. Read the category scores, and let the total be my problem.

And if you can afford two memberships, the answer is Surya plus House of Kairos. One for training, one for living.

## How I actually scored these

Most gym reviews you read in Bali are either a paid post or somebody's mate doing them a favour. So before I tear into anyone, here is exactly how the numbers were made.

Fourteen categories, each scored out of 5. Then weighted, because not everything matters equally.

**Weighted at 1.4x, the stuff that actually matters:**

- Equipment
- Gym space, meaning layout, size and changing rooms
- Hygiene
- Recovery

**Weighted at 1.2x, the stuff we all pretend does not matter:**

- Location
- Restaurant
- Vibes

**Weighted at 1x:**

- Extras, meaning co working, run clubs and events
- Building quality and style
- Combat
- Community
- Pool
- Classes, meaning the breadth and specialisation of each gym's published class timetable
- Price, meaning the standard published monthly membership rate, where cheaper scores higher

That gives a maximum of 81 points, which I have converted to a score out of 10 so it reads like a normal review. Because a couple of gyms land within a rounding error of each other, the raw points are in the table too.

Three things worth saying before you argue with me.

**The pool is not recovery, so I stopped scoring them together.** Recovery is the ice bath, the sauna, the steam room, the cold plunge. The stuff that is actually doing something to your body after you train. A pool is a pool. It is lovely, it is why half of Canggu picks a gym, and it belongs on the scorecard. But it belongs at 1x with the other nice to haves, rather than sitting at 1.4x pretending to be a recovery protocol. Splitting them cost three gyms a point of recovery they were only holding because they had a nice pool.

**Vibes and community are different things.** Vibes is how a place feels while you are in it: the smell, the sound, the light, the look. Community is whether you belong. Whether anyone learns your name, whether it is easy to make friends, whether you would still turn up if you had nobody to meet. A gym can nail one and miss the other completely, and two of the gyms above do exactly that, in opposite directions.

**Changing rooms sit inside gym space, at the heaviest weighting.** You spend real time in there, usually at your least comfortable, and it is the most reliable tell of whether a gym actually respects the people paying it.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best gym in Canggu?**

On a weighted score across fourteen categories, Omni, at 8.7 out of 10. It is the only gym in Canggu with no weak category. It scores a perfect 5 on space, hygiene and recovery, three of the four most heavily weighted categories, plus a 5 for classes. Reload is a close second at 7.8, with House of Kairos and Rite Gym locked together for third and fourth, separated by just 0.2 of a point once price is counted.

**Which Canggu gym has the best pool?**

House of Kairos, the only 5 for the pool on this list. It is the one pool here worth planning an afternoon around, and it comes with the best loungers too. Omni and Reload both score a 4. Everywhere else is a 3.

**Which gym in Canggu has the best recovery?**

Omni and Nirvana, both on a 5. Rite Gym in Pererenan scores a 4 but has the most considered recovery experience of any of them, with a secluded ice bath, calm music and essential oils in the steam room. The pool is scored separately here, so these are recovery facilities in the strict sense.

**Which Canggu gym has the best community?**

Nirvana, the only 5 for community on this list. It finishes sixth overall because it is a weak weight training gym, but if you are new to Bali and want somewhere it is easy to make friends, it is the best answer here. Omni, Reload and Obsidian II all score 4.

**Which Bali gym has the best equipment?**

Reload, comfortably. The machines there are the best on the island. Obsidian II and Undefeated are the next tier.

**Which is the best gym in Bali for boxing and muay thai?**

Of the eight gyms on this list, Surya Gym, and it is not close. It is the only one scoring a perfect 5 for combat, with a full fight club programme, a large mat area and its own octagon. To be plain about it, though, this list covers gyms, and dedicated fight schools are a different animal. If your goal is to train with the best fighters on the island, specialist gyms such as Soma and Bali MMA are simpler, grittier and better at that specific job. Surya is the best option if you want a proper gym wrapped around a serious fight programme. Rite Gym has the best single boxing room, though it also gets used for yoga.

**Which Canggu gym has the best changing rooms?**

None of them are good, which is the running theme of this review. Omni and House of Kairos are the cleanest. Surya Gym and Rite Gym are the two worst offenders, and both lose real points for it.

**Which Canggu gym has the best food?**

Reload and Obsidian II, the only two 5s for the restaurant on this list. Reload is one of the better places to eat in the area whether or not you trained. Obsidian II's kitchen is the one part of that gym that does not feel like an afterthought. Rite Gym is the worst, at a 2.

**Which Canggu gym has the best class schedule?**

Omni and Rite Gym share the top score of 5, for different reasons. Omni, for the co working space. House of Kairos is a close second if you care more about the room than the desk setup.
