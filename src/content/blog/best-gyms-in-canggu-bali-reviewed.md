## How I actually scored these

Every gym review you read in Bali is either a paid post or somebody's mate doing them a favour. So before I tear into anyone, here is exactly how the numbers were made.

Fourteen categories, each scored out of 5. Then weighted, because not everything matters equally.

**Weighted at 1.4x, the stuff that actually matters:**

- Equipment
- Gym space, meaning layout, size and changing rooms
- Hygiene
- Recovery

**Weighted at 1.2x, the stuff we all pretend does not matter:**

- Location
- Restaurant
- Vibes

**Weighted at 1x:**

- Extras, meaning co working, run clubs and events
- Building quality and style
- Combat
- Community
- Pool
- Classes, meaning the breadth and specialisation of each gym's published class timetable
- Price, meaning the standard published monthly membership rate, where cheaper scores higher

That gives a maximum of 81 points, which I have converted to a score out of 10 so it reads like a normal review. Because a couple of gyms land within a rounding error of each other, I have put the raw points in the table too.

Eight things worth saying before the list.

**The pool is not recovery, so I stopped scoring them together.** Recovery is the ice bath, the sauna, the steam room, the cold plunge. The stuff that is actually doing something to your body after you train. A pool is a pool. It is lovely, it is why half of Canggu picks a gym, and it belongs in the scorecard, but it belongs at 1x alongside the other nice to haves rather than sitting at 1.4x pretending to be a recovery protocol. Splitting them cost three gyms a point of recovery they were only holding because they had a nice pool.

**Vibes and community are not the same thing either.** Vibes is how a place feels while you are in it. The smell, the sound, the light, the aesthetic. Community is whether you belong. Whether anyone learns your name, whether it is easy to make friends, whether you would still turn up if you had nobody to meet. A gym can nail one and completely miss the other, and two of the gyms below do exactly that in opposite directions.

**Nothing gets counted twice, with one deliberate exception.** That is the rule that runs through this whole scorecard. A busy, sociable room scores under community and does not also inflate vibes. A nice pool scores under pool and does not also inflate recovery. The exception is Surya Gym, where the class timetable and the fight programme are the same thing. Its combat score and its classes score are both drawing on that one fight club, because for a gym whose entire schedule is boxing and muay thai, those really are the same asset viewed two ways rather than two separate strengths.

**Changing rooms sit inside gym space, at the heaviest weighting.** You spend real time in there, usually at your least comfortable, and it is the single most reliable tell of whether a gym actually respects the people paying it.

**Combat is only weighted at 1x and it still swings the mid table harder than almost anything else.** It has the widest spread of anything here, a perfect 5 down to an actual zero.

**Classes is scored differently to most of the others, and I want to be upfront about that.** Everything else in this list comes from actually training there. I have not sat through enough classes at all eight gyms to fairly judge whether an instructor is any good, so this category is scored on the breadth and specialisation of each gym's published timetable instead, how much is on offer and whether a gym is trying to do one thing well or everything reasonably. It says nothing about whether the classes themselves are good. And because gym timetables in Bali live half on outdated websites and half on Instagram Stories that vanish after 24 hours, treat this one column as a snapshot pulled in early September 2026 rather than something that will still be true by Christmas.

**Price is also scored differently, and it runs backwards to everything else.** Every other category rewards more. Price rewards less, because a lower monthly membership fee is a genuine point in a gym's favour rather than a compromise. I pulled the standard published monthly rate for six of these gyms straight from their own websites. Reload and House of Kairos do not publish a monthly rate anywhere, so those two are scored on direct enquiry rather than a public page, and I have placed them alongside Omni based on what I was told. Surya Gym and Nirvana both run several membership tiers at different prices, so I used the tier that matches what everyone else here bundles as standard, VIP for Surya and Life for Nirvana, rather than the cheapest option either one offers. Prices in Bali move often and without much notice, so treat this column the same way as classes, a snapshot from early September 2026 rather than something fixed.

**Ties break on equipment, then combat.** If two gyms land on the exact same number of points, the better actual gym wins, so I break it on equipment first and combat second. Nobody has needed it so far, though a couple of gyms below have come close enough on points to round to the same score out of 10, which is exactly why the points column sits next to it.

## 8. Undefeated, 6.3/10

Undefeated sits in Canggu, and the location is the least convenient on this list along with Nirvana. That is the worst thing I am going to say about it, so bear with me.

The building is a good one and the look is the whole personality of the place. Anime themed artwork throughout, done properly rather than done as a gimmick. It is a cool, quirky gym and it has an identity, which is rarer around here than it should be. That is why the vibes score holds at a 4 while gyms twice its size score lower.

The machines are great. Exactly what you need to train hard and nothing you do not. Along with Reload and Obsidian, this is the better equipment tier. The space is the smallest here, which is the honest reason it scores a 3 rather than anything to do with how it is laid out. Hygiene is solid. And then the recovery, which is the bit that surprised me. I heard "indoor recovery" and thought, what? In Bali? Then I saw it and stopped cringing immediately, because it works. The pool is a 3, which is to say it exists and it is fine.

There are no classes here at all. I looked everywhere, the website, Instagram, a straight search, nothing. It is a weights and machines operation and nothing else, and given everything I have already said about this place, that tracks. It costs Undefeated a full category on the scorecard, but it is consistent with the identity rather than a contradiction of it.

On price, this is not close. Undefeated is the cheapest gym on this entire list by a wide margin, at roughly 1,500,000 IDR a month before tax and service. If value for money is genuinely the priority rather than an afterthought, this is where that argument gets made.

Here is the key thing about Undefeated. It is never rammed. On a Tuesday at 5pm that is worth more than a smoothie bar. The flip side is a community score of 2, the lowest here. A quiet room is a lovely place to train and a hard place to make friends. You will get your session done and you will probably leave without speaking to anybody.

On the food and the rest of it, do not come here expecting either. The restaurant is fine and there is no co working space worth the name. It is not trying to do that, and I respect it for not pretending.

It finishes last because the scoring rewards breadth and Undefeated deliberately has none, and classes is still the clearest single example of that. Price is the opposite story, the highest score anywhere on this list, and the reason it is not further back still. If you want a focused room, good iron, no queue and the cheapest membership in Canggu, this is arguably the best value proposition here.

**Score:** Equipment 4, Space 3, Hygiene 4, Recovery 4, Location 3, Restaurant 3, Vibes 4, Extras 2, Building 4, Combat 2, Community 2, Pool 3, Classes 0, Price 5.

## 7. Obsidian II, 7.2/10

The location is good and easy, no complaints there.

The building is where it starts to go wrong. It has no taste. There is no design idea holding the place together, and the bits that have been added feel bolted on rather than planned. Chief among them, they added a water slide. A water slide. Who is using that? Which member walked in, saw a water slide, and thought yes, this is the gym for me. Vibes scores a 2, the lowest in that category on this list, and the building a 3.

Now the good part, and it is genuinely good. The equipment is excellent, one of the best sets of iron in Bali. The space itself is big, plenty of room to move heavy things around. The changing rooms are the letdown though, fine and nothing more, the kind of space you use and forget about five minutes later, which is what pulls the combined score for space down to a 3 rather than higher. Then the recovery, which is also a 3 and the joint weakest here. It is uninviting, which is a strange thing to build and then not make people want to use. Splitting the pool out into its own category is what exposed that, because the water slide was never recovery and now it is not scored as though it were. The pool itself is a 3.

Classes are one of the least documented things about this gym. The only two class types named anywhere online are Hyrox and yoga, and neither comes with an actual timetable I could find, on the website or through ClassPass. It may well run a bigger programme than that in practice, but nothing beyond those two names is publicly confirmed, so the score reflects what I could actually verify rather than what might be happening on the floor.

The key point is that this is a hardcore gym goer's gym and it knows it. The community is a genuine 4, which surprises people. Serious rooms tend to attract serious regulars, and regulars recognise each other.

The restaurant, though, is genuinely excellent, one of only two 5s for food on this list alongside Reload. It is the one part of Obsidian II that does not feel bolted on, somebody clearly cares about that kitchen even if nobody cared about the water slide. The extras beyond that are thin, and the rest of it still comes off a bit tacky overall. I want to be fair here though, so I am going to visit again before I lock the rest of this score in.

Price does not help either. At roughly $172 a month it sits toward the top of the pricing table, more expensive than everywhere except the very top tier, which is a tough spot for a gym already fighting a space score and a vibes score this low.

Seventh this time, just 0.2 of a point behind Nirvana. Price is a big part of why that gap stayed tight rather than closing altogether, Obsidian II is the more expensive of the two, and it is still not quite enough to cover for a space score that a great restaurant could not fully make up for either.

**Score:** Equipment 5, Space 3, Hygiene 4, Recovery 3, Location 4, Restaurant 5, Vibes 2, Extras 3, Building 3, Combat 4, Community 4, Pool 3, Classes 3, Price 4.

## 6. Nirvana, 7.2/10

The location is awful. It sits right in the tourist part, which is exactly why it is permanently busy, but getting there is a chore and getting out of the car park is worse.

The building is nothing special either. There is no architectural idea here, no material anybody agonised over. It is a room. Both of those scores are a 3 and neither of them is what people come for.

The equipment is the weakest on this list. Enough to train, not enough to progress on if you are serious about lifting. The space is decent and hygiene is fine. The recovery, though, is genuinely great and one of only two 5s in that category on this list. It survived the split from the pool intact, which is more than three other gyms managed. The pool itself is an ordinary 3.

Classes are the other place Nirvana is genuinely exceptional, just not in the direction you might expect. It runs more sessions a week than anywhere else on this list, comfortably over a hundred, with real depth across vinyasa, power, yin, hatha and gentle flow yoga, pilates, sculpt, barre, splits, handstands, and dance styles nothing else here touches, contemporary and hip hop among them. What it does not have, anywhere on the timetable, is a single class branded HIIT, and nothing boxing or kickboxing adjacent either. That gap is why it lands on a 4 rather than a 5. Enormous variety, just all pointed the same direction.

And now the actual point of Nirvana. It has the best community of any gym in Canggu, the only 5 in that category on the entire list. If you have just landed, know nobody, and want a place where it is genuinely easy to make friends, this is the answer and the total score is irrelevant to you. But be clear about what it is. This is a social club that happens to own dumbbells. It is a place for classes and for fannying around, not a weight training gym. Its vibes score dropped to a 3 once community was pulled out into its own category, and that is the honest reading. What people love about Nirvana is the people. The room itself is unremarkable.

The restaurant is good and there is a decent programme of things going on around the gym, which feeds straight back into why the place is so sociable.

Price sits in the middle of the pack. The Life tier, the one that actually gets you the full class programme rather than just the pool and recovery side, runs to roughly 3,200,000 IDR a month, on par with Rite Gym and Surya rather than anywhere near Undefeated.

Sixth this time, just 0.2 of a point clear of Obsidian II now that price is counted, though it is still the class programme doing most of the work to get it there.

And still the correct choice for a lot of people reading this.

**Score:** Equipment 3, Space 4, Hygiene 4, Recovery 5, Location 3, Restaurant 4, Vibes 3, Extras 4, Building 3, Combat 2, Community 5, Pool 3, Classes 4, Price 3.

## 5. Surya Gym, 7.5/10

Decent location, and a properly built building. Solid, well made, no complaints about the fabric of the place at all, which is why building scores a 5.

What it is not is designed. Nobody has thought about the light or the sound or what walking in should feel like. Vibes is a 3, and for a gym this focused on the actual training that is a fair trade rather than a failure.

The equipment is a little old. Perfectly good, but a little old. Nothing about the machines is going to make you post a story. The space works and the layout is sensible. Then the changing rooms, which are grim. Dingy, and not clean enough for a gym charging what it charges in a climate like this. The rooftop recovery is a great touch and the sort of thing you only appreciate after you have used it a few times, though scored on its own merits rather than lumped in with the pool it settles at a 4 rather than a 5. The pool is a 3.

Here is the key point, and it is a big one. Surya has the best fight facilities in Bali and it is not close. Not "there is a bag in the corner" level. Proper equipment, proper space, an actual [fight club programme](https://www.suryagymbali.com/fight-club/). If you want to box or do muay thai seriously in Canggu, this is the conversation and everywhere else is a footnote. It is the only 5 for combat on this list and it deserves it.

The fight club programme is also the entire class schedule, and there is nothing else on it. Boxing, muay thai, K1 and sparring, running from six in the morning to nine at night most days, open gym blocks filling every gap in between. No yoga, no pilates, no general fitness class of any kind. It is a deep, specialised programme rather than a broad one, and it earns a 4 rather than the 5 Rite Gym and Omni get precisely because broad is not what it is trying to be. This is also the one place on the scorecard where combat and classes are drawing on the same underlying strength rather than two separate ones, as explained above.

On food and extras, this is where it thins out fast. The restaurant is fine. Outside the fight programme there are pool tables on the ground floor and not a lot more. No co working worth the name, no run club, nothing built around the gym rather than inside it. Scored honestly, extras is a 2, and that is what puts a gym this good at one thing down in fifth.

Price lands mid table too, in the same tier as Rite Gym and Nirvana. The VIP membership, the tier that actually matches what everyone else here bundles as standard, runs to roughly 3,200,000 IDR a month, though the gym only option undercuts almost everything else on this list if all you want is the weights room.

**Score:** Equipment 4, Space 4, Hygiene 4, Recovery 4, Location 4, Restaurant 4, Vibes 3, Extras 2, Building 5, Combat 5, Community 3, Pool 3, Classes 4, Price 3.

## 4. Rite Gym, 7.7/10

This was my personal favourite for years, so understand that everything below is said with affection.

Best location on the list. Very close to the beach in [Pererenan](/invest/pererenan/), which matters more than people admit when you are deciding whether to actually go at 6am. And architecturally it is one of the best gyms I have been in anywhere. Industrial brutalist done properly rather than done as a mood board, with the concrete and the proportions actually thought through.

The equipment is good without being remarkable, and the space is well laid out without quite being the best on this list. Then the changing rooms, which are too dark and too dirty. Dark colours are a bad choice for a bathroom. You need to see dirt easily, otherwise germs get missed and the hygiene etiquette goes with it. There are also no spray wipes on the gym floor, which in a hot country is not a small thing, and that is what drags hygiene down to a 3, and pulls the combined space score down to a 4 alongside it. The recovery is the only one on this list that is truly trying to be zen. Chill music, a secluded ice bath, and essential oils in the steam room, which should be mandatory by law in 2026. It scores a 4 rather than a 5 because the facilities themselves are smaller than what Omni and Nirvana offer, not because the thinking is worse. The pool is an ordinary 3 and nobody comes here for it.

Classes are where Rite quietly does more than almost anyone outside Omni. Boxing and muay thai for combat, HYROX and 5x5 metcon for strength, a proper calisthenics and handstand programme nothing else on this list runs, yoga and pilates morning and evening, even sound baths and breath work. It is a fraction of the volume Omni or Nirvana put on, somewhere around 45 sessions a week against Nirvana's hundred plus, but it touches every lane rather than living in one, which is exactly what this category is meant to reward. It is a 5, tied with Omni, and on its own it would have pushed Rite up the table. A more honest look at the changing rooms cancels most of that gain back out, which is why Rite settles in fourth, close to where it has always sat.

The key thing about Rite is that it is the only gym in Canggu where somebody has thought about what the place smells like. It gained the most from separating vibes and community and it deserved to, because on pure sensory terms it is the strongest room here. Rite have little details right. It is truly designed by gym goers. The boxing room is the best I have seen anywhere. And then they will run a yoga class in it, which defeats the entire point. They do the same thing upstairs, running classes in the space normal people are trying to train in.

Then the food, which is terrible. Very basic, and in the evening you get eaten alive by mosquitos while you eat it. It is the lowest restaurant score on this list by a distance.

Price sits mid table, roughly 3,200,000 IDR a month for the four week unlimited pass, tax included. Not cheap, but not out of line with Nirvana or Surya either, and nowhere near what Omni, Reload or Kairos are asking.

Renovations are underway. I will re review at Christmas and I expect this score to move.

**Score:** Equipment 4, Space 4, Hygiene 3, Recovery 4, Location 5, Restaurant 2, Vibes 5, Extras 4, Building 5, Combat 4, Community 3, Pool 3, Classes 5, Price 3.

## 3. House of Kairos, 7.7/10

Great location, just down the road from Open House. And then the most beautiful gym in Bali. It is not close.

It resembles a Soho House more than a gym. Art deco through the restaurant and the co working space, genuinely stylish, with a level of finish nothing else here comes near. You feel good working out here. You feel like you are part of a luxury experience rather than a gym membership, and that is worth something.

The equipment is Technogym, so it is not the best iron on this list, but it is brand new Technogym and it is more than enough for 95% of people. The space is good and hygiene is a 5. The recovery is very good without being the best here, a 4, with cinnamon essential oil in the steam room, which is exactly the sort of decision that tells you somebody with taste is in charge. Then the small stuff lets it down slightly. There is no shower gel next to the shower. In a place this considered, that omission is loud.

And then the pool, which is the only 5 on this entire list. Best pool by a distance, and I mean that literally, it is the only pool here I actually want to swim in rather than photograph. Best loungers too. Separating the pool out of recovery cost most gyms a point and it is a big part of why Kairos still holds a place near the top of this list.

Classes are the other new wrinkle here, and I only found them because a reader sent me a screenshot of the actual timetable, which is members only and booked through the app rather than published anywhere I could search. It is a sensible little mix once you can see it: mobility, functional training, three yoga styles, pilates, hyrox and one circuit class, about thirteen sessions across five weekdays with nothing confirmed for the weekend. That is closer to a boutique studio bolted onto a members club than a full gym class programme, and next to what Omni or Reload run, it reads thin.

The key point, and it is the big one. No combat. Zero. Sometimes you just want to hit the bags for a while, burn some extra calories, hone a bit of technique. You cannot. The community is also only a 3. It is a beautiful room full of people having a nice time separately, which is not the same as belonging somewhere.

The restaurant is good and the co working space is stylish and usable. Classes are the reason Kairos drops from second to third on this update, and it is worth being honest about why. Everything else on this list moved because of things I could see and touch. This moved because of a category I could not train through, on a schedule so private I only found it because someone else had it on their phone. Kairos is still the most beautiful gym here, still owns the only 5 for pool, still scores an actual zero for combat, none of that changed. What changed is that Reload simply runs a bigger class programme, and once that started counting, Kairos slipped behind it by 0.8 of a point.

Price is the other reason this spot got interesting. Kairos does not publish a monthly rate anywhere, so this one is scored on what I was told directly rather than a page I can link to, and I have placed it alongside Omni in the pricier tier. It is also most of the reason the gap to Rite Gym in fourth closed to just 0.2 of a point. Rite pays less for its membership and very nearly climbed back past Kairos on that alone.

**Score:** Equipment 4, Space 4, Hygiene 5, Recovery 4, Location 5, Restaurant 4, Vibes 5, Extras 4, Building 5, Combat 0, Community 3, Pool 5, Classes 3, Price 2.

## 2. Reload, 7.8/10

Great location, easy to get to, and then a building that nobody designed with much care. The look is the weakest part of the whole operation. Glaring blue lights in the evening, bad artwork, and a layout that feels assembled rather than planned.

That is also where separating vibes and community really bites. Strip out the crowd and what is actually left is those blue lights and that artwork. Vibes is a 3, and it was only ever a 4 because the room is full.

Now the good part. The machines are the best of any gym in Bali, full stop. If you care about the quality of the iron more than anything else, you go here and you stop reading. The roof space is good and the room is big. The showers are shoved out the back near the bins, which is a bit grim. And the recovery area already looks like it is coming apart, which is remarkable given the place is not old. That is a 3 and it is the joint weakest recovery on this list. The pool, on the other hand, is a proper 4 and a big part of why people actually spend their afternoons here.

Classes are a genuine strength too, if a slightly chaotic one. Hyrox most days, boxing, one session actually called Combat Conditioning, a serious pilates rotation running across four different formats, and enough yoga and mobility work scattered through the week to round it out. It does not go as deep in any single lane as Rite Gym's calisthenics work or Nirvana's yoga catalogue, but the spread is real, and it is a 4 rather than a token gesture at having a timetable.

The key thing about Reload is that it is by far the busiest gym in Bali and I have never fully worked out why. All those people do add up to something real though, which is why community scores a 4.

The restaurant is fantastic, genuinely one of the better places to eat in the area regardless of whether you trained, and it is the only 5 for food on this list. Honestly, Reload is a beach club you can train at. Take it on those terms and it is excellent.

Price does not do Reload any favours either. There is no published monthly rate anywhere, so this is scored on direct enquiry same as Kairos, and it lands in the same expensive tier as Omni. Everything about this place, the food, the crowd, the machines, comes at a cost, and the price score is the one honest reminder of that.

Once classes were added to the scorecard, Reload jumped from third to second, landing 6.8 points behind Omni and 0.8 clear of House of Kairos for the spot. It earns that jump as much at the bar as it does on the class timetable. They are expanding, so who knows what turns up next.

**Score:** Equipment 5, Space 4, Hygiene 5, Recovery 3, Location 5, Restaurant 5, Vibes 3, Extras 3, Building 4, Combat 3, Community 4, Pool 4, Classes 4, Price 2.

## 1. Omni, 8.7/10

The location is good rather than perfect, and the building is excellent. Stylish without being smug, which is a harder balance to strike around here than it sounds. The vibes in the evening are the other thing. Some gyms empty out and die at 6pm. Omni gets better, and that is the lighting and the music as much as the people.

The equipment is a 4, so it is not the best iron in Bali and I am not going to pretend otherwise. Everything else in the heavy weighted block is perfect. Space 5, hygiene 5, recovery 5. Nothing else on this list manages that, and those are three of the four categories that count for the most. The infrared sauna is a genuine differentiator rather than a marketing line, and there are two outdoor training areas, which in this climate is the correct decision that almost nobody else makes. The pool is a solid 4, second only to Kairos.

Omni is also the only gym near the top of this list whose recovery score survived the pool being pulled out of it. That is the whole argument for splitting them. Its 5 was always about the sauna and the facilities, not about having somewhere nice to lie down.

Classes are the same story as everything else here, no weak spot. More than 65 sessions a week, and it is the only gym on this list that genuinely covers every base: HIIT, HYROX, boxing, kickboxing, deep mobility work, several yoga styles, dance, breathwork, even dedicated lap swim lanes four times a day. It is the only 5 alongside Rite Gym, and unlike Rite, it gets there on sheer volume as well as spread.

The key point about Omni is simply that it never gives you a reason to complain, which sounds like faint praise until you try to find another gym here you can say it about. Every other gym on this list has a hole in it somewhere. Reload has the recovery and the showers. Kairos has no combat and a thin class programme. Surya has the changing rooms and nothing outside the fighting. Rite has the food and the hygiene. Omni has a combat score of 3 and a price tag to match its ambitions, and that is the whole list of complaints.

And the co working area is the best in Canggu. I mean that flatly. Not the best gym co working area, the best co working area. If you train in the morning and then work until two, the maths on an Omni membership starts looking silly in your favour. The restaurant is good, the community is a 4, and the extras column is the strongest here.

Price is the one place Omni does not lead. It sits in the same expensive tier as Reload and Kairos, and if a monthly budget is the deciding factor rather than an afterthought, that is worth knowing before anything else on this page.

It wins by 6.8 points, the second largest gap on this table after the one separating seventh place from last, because it is still the only gym on this list that is bad at nothing.

**Score:** Equipment 4, Space 5, Hygiene 5, Recovery 5, Location 4, Restaurant 4, Vibes 5, Extras 5, Building 5, Combat 3, Community 4, Pool 4, Classes 5, Price 2.

## So which one should you actually join

- **You want the fewest compromises:** Omni. It is first for a reason, and by the biggest margin near the top of the table.
- **You have just arrived and know nobody:** Nirvana. Seventh on this list and the correct answer for you by a mile.
- **You fight, or you want to:** Surya Gym. Not a debate. Bring your own flip flops.
- **You train in the morning and work all afternoon:** Omni. The co working alone justifies it.
- **You actually swim:** House of Kairos, then Omni or Reload. Nothing else here has a pool worth planning your day around.
- **You want to feel like your life is going well:** House of Kairos.
- **You only care about the machines:** Reload, then Obsidian II, then Undefeated.
- **You want the widest class timetable:** Omni or Rite Gym, both a 5 for classes, though Omni has far more volume behind it.
- **You live in Pererenan:** Rite Gym, fourth on this list, and the renovation may well push it higher by Christmas.
- **You want to train hard, cheap, with no queue:** Undefeated.
- **You want the cheapest membership on the list:** Undefeated, by a wide margin, then Obsidian II.

Which is the real lesson of this list. The winner is the gym with no weaknesses, but the right gym for you is almost always the one that is brilliant at the single thing you actually care about. Nirvana is seventh and it is the best answer for a lot of people reading this. Read the category scores, not the total.

And if you can afford two memberships, the honest answer that nobody wants to hear is Surya plus House of Kairos. One for training, one for living.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best gym in Canggu?**

On a weighted score across fourteen categories, Omni at 8.7 out of 10. It is the only gym in Canggu with no weak category, and it scores a perfect 5 on space, hygiene and recovery, three of the four heaviest weighted categories, plus a 5 for classes. Reload is a close second at 7.8, with House of Kairos and Rite Gym locked together for third and fourth, separated by just 0.2 of a point once price is counted.

**Which Canggu gym has the best pool?**

House of Kairos, and it is the only 5 for the pool on this list. It is the one pool here worth planning an afternoon around, and it comes with the best loungers too. Omni and Reload both score a 4. Everywhere else is a 3.

**Which gym in Canggu has the best recovery?**

Omni and Nirvana, both on a 5. Rite Gym in Pererenan scores a 4 but has the most considered recovery experience of any of them, with a secluded ice bath, calm music and essential oils in the steam room. Note that the pool is scored separately here, so these are recovery facilities in the strict sense.

**Which Canggu gym has the best community?**

Nirvana, and it is the only 5 for community on this list. It finishes sixth overall because it is a weak weight training gym, but if you are new to Bali and want somewhere it is easy to make friends, it is the best answer here. Omni, Reload and Obsidian II all score 4.

**Which Bali gym has the best equipment?**

Reload, comfortably. The machines there are the best on the island. Obsidian II and Undefeated are the next tier.

**Which is the best gym in Bali for boxing and muay thai?**

Surya Gym, and it is not close. It is the only gym here scoring a perfect 5 for combat, with a proper fight club programme rather than a bag in the corner. It finishes fifth overall only because it offers very little outside the fighting. Rite Gym has the best single boxing room, though it also gets used for yoga classes.

**Which Canggu gym has the best changing rooms?**

None of them are good, which is the running theme of this review. Omni and House of Kairos are the cleanest. Surya Gym and Rite Gym are the two worst offenders and both lose real points for it.

**Which Canggu gym has the best food?**

Reload and Obsidian II, the only two 5s for the restaurant on this list. Reload is genuinely one of the better places to eat in the area whether or not you trained. Obsidian II's kitchen is the one part of that gym that does not feel like an afterthought. Rite Gym is the worst at a 2.

**Which Canggu gym has the best class schedule?**

Omni and Rite Gym share the top score of 5, for different reasons. Omni runs more than 65 sessions a week and covers essentially every style going, from HIIT and HYROX to boxing, kickboxing, yoga and dance. Rite Gym runs a fraction of that volume but touches combat, strength, calisthenics and yoga without leaning too hard on any single one of them. Nirvana actually runs the single largest class programme of any gym here, well over a hundred sessions a week, but it is almost entirely yoga, pilates and mobility, so it lands on a 4. Surya Gym is the opposite kind of specialist, every class is boxing, muay thai or sparring, nothing else exists. These are pulled from each gym's published timetable in early September 2026 and will drift over time, so treat this one as a snapshot rather than gospel.

**Which Canggu gym is best for digital nomads?**

Omni, for the co working space. House of Kairos is a close second if you care more about the environment than the desk setup.

**How much do gyms cost in Canggu?**

Undefeated is the cheapest on this list by a wide margin, at roughly 1,500,000 IDR a month before tax and service. At the other end, Omni, Reload and House of Kairos all sit in the same expensive tier, with Omni's standard rate running to around 4,000,000 IDR a month. Reload and Kairos do not publish a monthly rate anywhere, so those two figures are estimated from direct enquiry rather than a public page, at a similar level to Omni. Rite Gym, Surya and Nirvana sit in the middle, all around 3,200,000 IDR a month for their standard membership. Prices here move often and without much warning, so treat this as a snapshot from early September 2026 rather than a fixed number.
